What is an offer?
Simply put: when you reach out to someone cold, they're inherently skeptical. In order to beat that skepticism, you need to offer something so good that they can't logically say no to it. Ideally this is considered very valuable by your prospect, is easy to say yes to, and doesn't take you very much time/money to put together for them.
If you don’t have an offer, your outreach will almost certainly perform poorly. The difference between a campaign with a good offer and one without any offer at all can be as high as 10x the reply rate. So don’t skip this step!
Here are some examples of offers:
•	When I was scaling 1SecondCopy to 7-figures, we ran a very simple offer: just send us a title, and we'll give you a completely free, 500-word blog post on the topic—no strings attached.
o	Because AI was not yet recognized as a writing tool, a 500-word blog post by a professional writer was considered very valuable. This let us get our foot in the door with many clients.
o	We also didn't ask for anything but a title. This made our offer easy to respond to, and since we made it clear there were no strings, logically there was no reason for people not to get back to us. Low friction!
o	We sent around 20,000 emails. Our initial campaign had a ~8% reply rate. As you can imagine, this was quite profitable.
•	When I started selling systems for LeftClick, I tested many straightforward offers. One of the winners was: I will guarantee you 20 booked sales appointments in the next 60 days or you don't pay—no strings attached. Just say yes and I'll get started.
o	Pretty much every service business struggles with lead generation. So this was a hot topic very near and dear to people's hearts. Like before, this let me get my foot in the door easily.
o	Since all I asked for was a "yes", it was easy for people to respond. I also made it clear there were no strings, gave definitive start and end dates (i.e the next 60 days), and made it basically free. Logically there's no reason why people wouldn't get back to me.
o	I ran many campaigns and variations of this offer, but the best had a 7% reply rate across a few hundred emails.
You can probably tell, but offers tend to be quite similar in nature.
How are offers structured?
A lot of people have different opinions on offers. Here is mine. You should structure your offer in a way that:
•	Minimizes financial risk—i.e, offers some sort of "free" or "money back guarantee" component.
•	Minimizes friction—i.e, lets your prospect start immediately with very little work on their end.
•	Doesn't cost you a lot to produce—i.e, since you're probably offering something for free, getting a lot of "yeses" won't put you out of business.
If that sounds complicated, you can usually get most of the way there with this formula:
I will give you [thing] in [time] or your money back—just send me [input].
There's no rule saying you have to develop your own proprietary offer, either. You can just copy someone else's (I certainly did when I started). You can also use an offer as inspiration, and then massage into your own business model with a couple of changes. Anything is valid.
